What are those?

July 31, 2026 4 min read Your Name

What are those?

But let’s start with some definitions - Governance, Risk, and Compliance, or GRC for short, is a vital aspect for any organization. In today's fast-paced business world, companies often face complex and varied risks, including legal compliance, data privacy, and information security.

What's interesting is that these risks can often multiply as organizations grow in size and complexity. But how much exactly? To what extend do we need to come up with tools just to control this complexity (GRC tools are quite complex)?

Human connection is quite complex also

(As if you don’t know this yet)

I work as a Project manager before, and if you really love this job you better read The Mythical Man-month, in which there is an important idea that wraps in a law.

Brooks' Law consolidates the observation about software project management and stated that adding manpower to a software project that is behind schedule will delays it even longer.

Quite contrary to what you think huh? More manpower doesn’t equal speed, it is due to many reasons:

  1. Ramp-up time - No one gets efficient right away

  2. Adding more people means you need more tasks at once to assign - But in software, there are a lot of dependencies a.k.a you have to wait in line to do the task, not at once.

    1. An excellent example here: while it takes one woman nine months to make one baby, "nine women can't make a baby in one month".

  3. And finally, communication overhead increases as the number of people grows

The third point is where it gets interesting and this applies to the organization also.

The number of different communication channels increases rapidly with the number of people.

Everyone working on the same task needs to keep in sync, so as more people are added they spend more time trying to find out what everyone else is doing.

This will also increase the total number of unknown factors and it harder to detect and analyze potential risks, the chances of risk to happen increase as more people and systems are involved.

So as organizations grow, the complexity of governing, managing, and remaining compliant with regulations increases exponentially.

But to what degree does the complexity grow?

We have another law (Wikipedia please sponsor me here!)

Netcalfe's Law on telecommunications network, Metcalfe's Law is related to the fact that the number of unique possible connections in a network of n nodes can be expressed mathematically as the n(n-1)/2, which is proportional to n square.

While not always on the exact number we can imagine the growth here.

As an example: Two telephones can make only one connection, five can make 10 connections, and twelve can make 66 connections.

We have around 15 billion mobile devices worldwide as this statistic indicated, imagine the number of connections.

In an organization that is big enough, the number of connections and channels of communication look quite messy. Alphabet (it’s Google FYI) has around 200,000 personnel, doing managing things in this company must be hectic.

This is where you need some control system to step in

These highlight the importance of GRC in today's digital world, adding the fact that for businesses operating in increasingly complex, geographically dispersed, and digitally connected environments, governing the risks of these networks is vital.

With the exponential increase in the number of events that can happen, organizations need to have better control systems and processes in place. This is where GRC comes in by providing a structured and coordinated approach to governance, risk management, and compliance, organizations can mitigate potential risks, protect their reputation, and ultimately succeed in their respective industries.

Best fit for a big corporation to manage, GRC also benefits some other types of business that require highly regulated industries, like finance, healthcare, oil and gas which require comprehensive compliance programs.

Risk management you can also name cybersecurity, supply chain, etc.

One other niche that you might overlook - is the companies that want M&A, IPO, essentially they will need it clean for due diligence.

Quite fascinating if you ask me.
