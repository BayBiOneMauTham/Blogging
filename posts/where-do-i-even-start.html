Where do I even start?

Just look at this equation, this and the question above actually come from a same concept of problem called Fermi problem, also know as an order-of-magnitude problem.

The differences is asking “How many orange you can fit unto a bus?” in an interview and you got called dumb interviewer, but getting the equation to answer the question “estimate the number of active, communicative extraterrestrial civilizations in the Milky Way Galaxy.” and you are one of the most intelligent person alive.

I do, personally, like the question in an interview, maybe I’m that dumb, you would never know.

So let’s take little steps back

“How many orange you can fit on a bus?” is actually asked in real-life from a company I know, not for me but for the engineers. I don’t really think the guy asking this question really know the concept of Fermi problem to be honest, the answer he give is “I want to know how you think”, reasonable or dumb, I don’t know. But if you are a down on earth engineer, you probably dislike this question, wondering how it impact your work.

Well, Chris put it well here, asking "how many golf balls fit on a bus” https://www.chrisstucchio.com/blog/2012/why_i_ask_fermi_problems.html

I don’t know who he is, an complete internet stranger, but he got a point though.

And then I got another question that I like during an interview, for Product manager role, the question is “estimate how many people travel to/from X (X is my city) airport a month”, okay I like it. These kind of questions repeatedly show up within 4-5 rounds of interviews and follow through, I’m not the most realistic guys out there, I got that.

And my answer?

I don’t remember the exact conversation it not something you got extra time to formulate you know, but the rough idea is I tried to understand the question first with some questions:

  • Is there any particular factors impact the number? I assume this is a typical month, no holiday, 30 days within that month, weather normal…. Something like that

  • The time range is close now, so the condition would be roughly normal? Political and such thing can impact the result, nothing got cancel etc..

  • In HCMC airport meaning they got in or out a door, typical stuff? Or any particular meaning to this, do this number including people working there (ask this along the formulation)…

  • Travel to/from meaning in and out the door count every time separately.

And then try to break down into a formula, as this make thing easier to visualize and used

  • With Total number of people as A

  • People work there as B

  • People travel there as C

  • And frequency as F (define as each time a person enter or exit via a door)

Then we got a simplest formulation

A = B*F1 + C*F2

This still very fundamental, so let’s drill down a bit further, for the people who work there

  • How to estimate the number of people working in the airport → I estimate via estimate the number of function (~10) and people of each function (~25 idk) for minimum number of person needed (B1) and how big the airport maximum capacity for employee, measure in number people of course (B2) is, roughly.

  • Assumes each person got in and out 4 times a day (F1) if they work full time, morning-lunch out- lunch in - got home. If they got cafeteria then this is equal out the outliner who out more.

So we got roughly the B and the F1 number. B become (B1+B2)/2*F1

Back to the people who travel, won’t include the people who stop there since they don’t travel in a country territory

  • Assumes that basis of flight set is diplomatic relation, if you have relation with a country you usually have flight there so naturally I’m back to a number I familiar with, roughly 180-190 countries.

    • Each flight, now this is tricky, I have no idea how many each person flight have, but calculated in the number of country that is far (>300km) they should use bigger airplane, 9-10 column * 50 rows = 500 seat per flight (C2 max)

    • For closer countries, around 20 maybe that is closer (<300km), 6 column * 30 rows = 180 seat per flight (C2 min)

    • so C = (C min*20 + C max*180)/2

  • Assumes that 2 flights per country per day, and they got out once per day (F2), major countries have more and minor countries might have 1 per week, factor in that number of developed countries < number of developing one (just common sense) so we have around 50-50 chance here this is a correct answer. Again purely assumption based on few evidences but have some true in it.

So we got C*F2 to (C min*20 + C max*180)/2*F2

Final answer was something like A = B*F1 + C*F2 = (B1+B2)/2 *F1+ (C max*180 + C min*20)/2*F2

So using my formula we got

  • My estimation 102,100 round of travel back and forth to X airport everyday.

  • Real number (with 20k passenger per day) using my formula ~ 44,000.

Drill a bit deeper:

  • Number of people working at the airport, average number in the US was 2474 per airport, I got 2125. Pretty close.

  • Real number of traveller is far different from my estimation, which is 46,800 vs 130k peak season and 20k in normal day.

  • So only close on the first half, which is an easier one.

Not bad for a thin air guess, huh?

💡An example is Enrico Fermi's estimate of the strength of the atomic bomb that detonated at the Trinity test, based on the distance traveled by pieces of paper he dropped from his hand during the blast. Fermi's estimate of 10 kilotons of TNT was well within an order of magnitude of the now-accepted value of 21 kilotons

Why this work for me.

The answer above is exactly why I dig these kind of questions, it weird and non-practical but got you thinking, a lot of thinking to get the closest number as possible or with plausible explanation.

For me that is a Product Manager job, we guess all the time, but the better we formulate our guesses, the better chance you convince other to do the work and yes, better the chance our company win on a long run.

Now, my answer is no where near the level of complexity or thinking that the great thinker have.

But similarly, for Drake equation problem (The screenshot equation above), the point was not for purposes of quantifying the number of civilizations, but as a way to stimulate scientific dialogue at the first scientific meeting on the search for extraterrestrial intelligence (SETI).

So if you don’t think it work, what your estimation?
