Couple of years ago

I don’t remember how far backward, I read “The last question“ by Issac Asimov, one of several stories that I read in one sitting, not because it was short, yes indeed length was a factor, but usually the reason is the book come with a question.

Good book make you think long after you read the last page, some engrave in your mind ideas, some made you who you are.

Consume order matter

I don’t like read and watch the same material, no no. This begin when I read Eragon in middle school and then watch the movie, one of these was shit, I’m not gonna tell which but surprise surprise, usually the later suck harder.

What come first already create an expectation in your mind (If it’s good enough), the fragility of that vision will soon be break once you in contact with another illustration of the idea, something in you know it isn’t right, odd, off the book of what you are expecting, and then naturally you reject the new presentation.

Even though I love both form of expression, I think a ground rule is need, for thing that I watched I don’t read and vice versa. Also apply to version if the art.

This work for LOTR, Dune, Full metal jacket, 2001 space odyssey, All quite on eastern front (I actually read before watching but never finished the book, lol), all the good books I missed since I watch movie more than I read (Call me lazy).

Then come “Story of your life”

is one of those book, it was the original base of “The arrival“ 2016, you know, the one with compelling story, great music and cinematography but was so lengthy you slept through it for the first few try, yeah, that kind of movie.

I watched the movie but then after seeing the concept present in the book, I tried to read it and it blow my mind.

Both version are nice but I do like the short story better, but without seeing the movie first, it’s very hard to figure what the book is trying to say. I’ll try to briefly go through the complexity of the concept here

Language define thinking, our language (both writing and speaking) is sequential because we experience the world (esp time) in chronological order.

Imagine if you understand the world as whole, a complete timeline back and forth, then your writing should be the same, you write the whole book in one single stroke. However, this doesn’t apply to speaking, as sound creation have physically limit, it usually be sequential even if you know the whole sentence.

Being able to know everything within a timeline at once lead to another concept, determinism paradox in time. If you know everything already, then you can change your future, if you change the future then all you know is not true, then you know nothing about that future to begin with.

=> That why I don’t trust fortune teller.

The story also delve into communication between species, how do we form our concept of “language“, as it just strokes in the eye of alien, or why “walking“ indicate the action of moving?

And the parent-children relationship, as parents usually the one who introduce language to their kids, along side their world view, as these must come together.

Here some key points of the two

The Arrival (cinema version)

  • Eerie, space alien called Heptapods that teach you language that change your perception

  • Focus on communication, cross species and between human with a touch on family side.

  • Vaguely present determinism-lity in time

  • More exciting story but lack of depth.

The Story of your life (Writing form)

  • Focus on linguistics and personal side of the character

  • Demonstrate more accurate via word the situation than visual (Now this is exceptional, explanation below)

  • Present determinism-lity in time in a good way

  • Deep but kinda boring story.

The exceptional of the writing form

The tenses of the text

The concept of both version present time as non-linear and one should know the end-to-end before start writing the Heptapods B language (Yes there are Heptapods A but that unrelated to B) so naturally our main character experience all the time frame at once if she deep into the language.

Heptapods A is speaking language, this is sequential and somehow similar to how human perceive language and the world.

Heptapods B is writing language, Heptapods B is holistic and present the entire idea at once stroke - which is very different from our perception of language or the world.

Heptapods B this doesn’t present any sound or related to Heptapods A. In fact, the point of using Heptapods B is to bypass the natural barrier of Heptapods A, in which sound need to be created in order.

Back to the writing for of our story, using text, the time is easily switch, you change past and future tense between paragraphs, this is very subtle, especially for a non-native reader like me, I understand English but not translate directly to the correct tense in my native language, usually I translate into present form and understand it as a past/future phase for the chapter.

In this book, it but in the middle of two part without waring, but hint that the switching context happen at night, when character sleep.

Here an example:

Weber considered it. For me it wasn’t even a question, but from his point of view it was a difficult one; like a soldier, though, he made it quickly. “Request granted. Talk to the sergeant outside about bringing in what you need. Have it ready for tomorrow.”

I remember one day during the summer when you’re sixteen. For once, the person waiting for her date to arrive is me. Of course, you’ll be waiting around too, curious to see what he looks like. You’ll have friend of yours, a blond girl with the unlikely name of Roxie, hanging out with you, giggling.

See the differences between the two parts? They were on the same page.

Watching the movie, this will be present more abruptly, in between and removing the subtlety of which the sentences provide, only one time the main characters talk to each others and ask if this happen often when she were slept.

The medium

But then everything would not make sense if you haven’t seen the movie, at least for me, the writing of the Heptapods B is, well, not that easy to understand in the book until you can visualize it, and it not meant for a textual explanation, as Heptapods B is logographic, which mean they don’t present sound but rather actual vision itself.

The movie, for me, convey fully the idea of the language represenatation and the medium we perceive it through, without the movie the language can never be completely understand.

The story line

The child and the husband are standing in the background more in the movie, make way for the conflict within human world, a choice that make sense if you doing a block buster but at the same time, take the humanity and evolvement of characters out of the story.

In the book, you would see the child more vivid, as she grow, learn and interact with her mom, she is the presentation of the language evolution in human, the way she learn to speak and the growth in complexity of her vocabulary (From mispronounce simple word to asking for a scientific term)

She also deepen the concept of determinism in time, as all the thing what will happens, either good or bad, will happens. Her mother accept it as part of the journey and raise the question that encapsulate the movie.

“If you could see your whole life from start to finish, would you change things?”.

The book also come with the reasoning that you don’t see in the movie.

The heptapods are neither free nor bound as we understand those concepts; they don’t act according to their will, nor are they helpless automatons. What distinguishes the heptapods’ mode of awareness is not just that their actions coincide with history’s events; it is also that their motives coincide with history’s purposes. They act to create the future, to enact chronology.

Freedom isn’t an illusion; it’s perfectly real in the context of sequential consciousness. Within the context of simultaneous consciousness, ুeedom is not meaningful, but neither is coercion; it’s simply a different context, no more or less valid than the other. It’s like that famous optical illusion, the drawing of either an elegant young woman, face turned away from the viewer, or a wart-nosed crone, chin tucked down on her chest. There’s no “correct” interpretation; both are equally valid. But you can’t see both at the same time.

Similarly, knowledge of the future was incompatible with free will. What made it possible for me to exercise freedom of choice also made it impossible for me to know the future. Conversely, now that I know the future, I would never act contrary to that future, including telling others what I know: those who know the future don’t talk about it. Those who’ve read the Book of Ages never admit to it.

If I could have described this to someone who didn’t already know, she might ask, if the heptapods already knew everything that they would ever say or hear, what was the point of their using language at all? A reasonable question. But language wasn’t only for communication: it was also a form of action. According to speech act theory, statements like “You’re under arrest,” “I christen this vessel,” or “I promise” were all performative: a speaker could perform the action only by uttering the words. For such acts, knowing what would be said didn’t change anything. Everyone at a wedding anticipated the words “I now pronounce you husband and wife,” but until the minister actually said them, the ceremony didn’t count. With performative language, saying equaled doing.

For the heptapods, all language was performative. Instead of using language to inform, they used language to actualize. Sure, heptapods already knew what would be said in any conversation; but in order for their knowledge to be true, the conversation would have to take place.

If you familiar with the branching concept of time, this will answer why this happen that way, not the other way around.

And in the story, the mommy and her child conversations are weaved deep into the concept.
