Culture-fit? Is that a real thing?

July 31, 2026 5 min read Your Name

Culture-fit? Is that a real thing?

Lately, I was building a new team, from ground up. Naturally, struggling with something people say a lot, but less than a handful can define it, hire culture-fit people.

And while that is not really need to be all 100% of your employee (You not gonna have time for that, trust me, I’ve been there), but it essential for the founding members early on, after all, you are not gonna go far if your co-pilot wanted thing a different ways.

But to be honest, what is a company culture? Google search (Or BambooHR to be specific) put it this way:

Company culture is a shared set of workplace beliefs, values, attitudes, standards, purposes and behaviors. It reflects both the written and unwritten rules that people in an organization follow.

Your organization's culture is the sum of all that you and your colleagues think, say, and do as you work together

So people will just getting together and create a culture? Just like that?

The truth is, human love meeting alike people, and we should be the one who we want to meet first. As this random science paper I found online stated, we tend to attract like-minded and that:

birds of a feather find each other before flocking.

We are that type of person already so we can get into that organization, not that we are inside so we becoming like others. Culture should be created by the founding members and reserve through rigidly hiring.

I’ve been in several companies, from big corporation to small start-up, and for me the above is true, any culture present should be felt through the air, you will know if you a fit right away. So take a trip to the company you applied should always be prioritized within hiring process.

So yeah,

  1. Culture-fit is real (Need more science on this, for now it good enough to get you thinking);

  2. We should get people that fit not try to change them;

  3. Visiting should be an early priority during the hiring process.

What culture you are building reflect back.

Ultra Rapid Fire or URF is something League of Legends (Yeah, the game where you feed and blame others) invented, it’s a crazy, mess up and SONIC-FAST mode. I like it.

Some brother in tech got the same idea too, they went far to the point setting a company and make speed as part of their culture.

They’re called the Collison brothers, Patrick and John. (They move so fast they last name almost Collision). Patrick also have a blog listing thing that been done fast, a sheer dedication for what he believed.

Stripe went deep by moving fast. Being deep does not mean moving slowly. Actually, Stripe is explicitly focused on speed. As John Collison explains, “speed is of the essence, and a defensible trait in companies.”

Stripe publishes updates to its core API 16 times per day.

Other companies would have fired several guys doing that.

I do like the idea. So building the culture for my team, I put speed first (Plus precision or at least learning from your mistake fast enough, otherwise you will crash and burn), the fast-mover, learner, adapter are welcome.

The culture you build reflect back on your self. So next time finding a company to apply, watch for the founders, what they say and how they act. I join a company for free (as a part time Product guy) because I believe that the founders have philosophies match what they do.

And a short how-to

Knowing what the culture you want, and who you are, now let’s start building stuff.

Disclaimer: If you do this and fail, it not on me alright. And these are also noob advices, so if you got better ideas, please lemme know.

Like as said, it’s something you feel, so don’t create check lists, go talk to the guy/girl, 1 on 1 with them and let’s them talk. Ask them questions, as a friend, as someone that they can be the true-selves with.

Or not talking at all, I like doer not talker, I’m have enough stories to tell already. Have a probation phase where you observe and ask others how they feel on the new-in-town, this especially true if you personally know the person in real life (not work-life), personal bias can sometime cloud your judgment, nice doesn’t equal workable. Always ask for opinions (360 with scope this time), if you ask enough there will be a pattern soon if he/she not a fit.

After that, be constructive, everyone deserve a second chance, if they didn’t make the cut, try again. But be honest, tell them the truth is helping.

There will be good and bad ends for different people, not that they ain’t great, just they don’t fit here. Always be nice, my peers, my managers, my C-suites, sometime the one who doesn’t fit is you (guess it’s time to be a FAST mover, true every time).

TL-DR:

  1. Culture-fit is real (Need more science on this, for now it good enough to get you thinking);

  2. We should get people that fit not try to change them;

  3. Visiting should be an early priority during the hiring process;

  4. Let’s interviewee talk and express them self or observe what they do if you have time;

  5. Give everyone a second change.
