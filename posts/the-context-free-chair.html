The context-free chair

Let’s start with a chair,

The Mono Block Plastic Chair is an unassuming household object that has become ubiquitous. They can be found in homes, schools, and offices all around the world. This seemingly simple object, however, has an backstory that spans multiple continents, highlighting the complex global economy and the rise of mass-produced goods.

The Mono Block Plastic Chair is the embodiment of mass produced goods, without any traits of individualism, created by using a single mold. It’s a context-free object, it’s a everywhere, everything, all at once multiverse thingy.

You can read more on it in this blog.

The manufacture of this chair is a global endeavor. The raw materials used to create the chair are sourced from various countries, including oil from the Middle East, and petrochemicals from North America. These materials are then shipped to factories in Asia, where they are molded and assembled into the chairs. Afterward, the chairs are distributed worldwide, with billions produced each year.

But there are still luxury chairs that cost a lot more than I make yearly…

Luxury is created not given.

Contrary to the Mono Block Plastic Chair, we got Audemars Piguet’s Royal Oak (One of the You-ain’t-that-rich-if-you-haven’t-heard-of-this thing).

By today’s standards, the Royal Oak is a total success and one of the most iconic watch designs of all time. But AP wasn’t all that fancy from the beginning.

I love watches, some of them. And realistically saying, quartz wristwatch was a game changer, it’s precise engineering victory (vibrate exactly 32768 times per second), and more important, can be mass-produced cheaply.

Begin around 1960s, Japan was in rapid industrialization mode (they lost a war and intended to win the next). Soon after the Swiss came up with their prototype, the Japanese invented a mass production system for quartz watches.

More detail here. I ain’t expert in this.

By 1980, Japan had surpassed Switzerland to become the top watch manufacturing nation. Japan’s success brought its watchmakers — like Seiko Holdings Corporation (TYO: 8050) and Casio Computer Co Ltd (TYO: 6952) onto the world stage — while crippling the Swiss watchmaking industry. Between 1970 and 1980, Switzerland lost 60% of its watchmaking companies and two-thirds of its workforce.

Then come the Swatch, yeah, those cheap-plastic-colorful watch I had when I was a kid, when everyone was thinking owning a Swatch meaning own a piece of Swiss watch-maker heritage. I was dead wrong.

Finally, some of the Swiss watch makers at the time like AP and Rolex say something like “enough is enough“ and they go for not watch-making but luxury-making business. The war that between hand-craft luxury and mass-produced begin.

The Swiss essentially gave birth to the concept of “luxury watches” and the rest is history.

AI-generated, that how you gonna lose your job.

After the rise of the Mono Block Plastic Chair, Swatch. Lately, there has been a significant shift towards mass-produced, artificially intelligent-generated content, art pieces. With recent developments in machine learning and AI, it is now possible for computers to generate written or visual content, such as blog posts or pictures, quickly and affordably.

As you might guess by now, there will be segments for hand-craft luxury and mass-produced. Some have already begin to utilized AI for art selling on Etsy. Not that easy, but a lot easier compare to the several years ago.

So what’s now?

Personally, I believe in the next power house baron that own a production line will lead the industry, from the chair to watches not AI-generated content, who ride the wave early survived and thrived. But what do i know? Luxury watches are still in high demand, might be higher than ever, it was the most valued investment during the pandemic.

How about you, designers/artists/content-writer? Where will you be in the next 5 years?

There will be new jobs, new type of jobs. Ride the waves and learn the stuff before too late or stay niche. Be the hand-craft luxury artist or be the mass-produced production house baron, pick a Pokemon, and fast.
