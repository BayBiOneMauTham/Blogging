The moment and place that the phenomenon of human government developed is lost in time; however, history does record the formations of early governments. The "founder's dilemma"

The "founder's dilemma" refers to the critical challenges and choices that entrepreneurs face as they transition from being the founder of a startup to potentially relinquishing control of the company. The dilemma typically arises when founders must decide between maximizing their financial gains ("rich") or retaining control over their company ("king").

It was written by Noam Wasserman.

Some cases of transition have been noted in the book

  • Google: Larry Page and Sergey Brin hired Eric Schmidt as CEO to bring in experienced leadership while they focused on product development.

  • Twitter: Jack Dorsey stepped down as CEO multiple times to bring in professional management.

  • Apple: Steve Jobs was famously ousted from Apple, only to return years later and lead the company to unprecedented success.

It’s reasonable for a founder to want to maintain control over their company, making key decisions and preserving their original vision. This case is usually right for early stage start up founder, when you know what you are doing with clear vision end-to-end.
