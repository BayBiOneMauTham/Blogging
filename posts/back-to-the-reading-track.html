Back to the reading track

July 31, 2026 5 min read Your Name

Too much for PM-ing, getting back on the normally random stuff I guess

Back to the reading track

I have always loved to read and read everything I can in between working hours (let’s just say sometimes during also). The feeling of scrolling through Wikipedia just to read something or find something fun enough to read is just too good, I guess.

But with highly stressed work, at least for my last job — I gradually dropped books or anything longer than 1000 words at a time. I don’t know, but 1000 seems like a big enough number to summarize how exhausted I was.

With some time on my hands now and a little help from a friend (Insert the Beatle song here) at work, I began again.

An Elegant Puzzle

To pick between a library of 100-ish books (Again an estimation without any data backup, you are welcome), but dang, the library was big. Most of them were for product managers, entrepreneurs - Gosh, never remember how to write that word properly, econ, and sometimes life itself.

So to solve the puzzle of “what should I read first”, I started with “An elegant puzzle - System of engineering management” - ba-dum-tss.

For no special reasons other than wanting to know If is there any way better than what I did recently to adjust how we roll in a company (spoiler alert, yes, but not much).

  • You would prob want to read this book if you are in a position that can change things, and WANT to change things, as I always emphasize later, enough motivation will get you far.

New and fresh, published around 2019 by a bald dude (Bald dude rule) who happens to be a high-level manager at Stipe (Yay, love Stripe), Uber, Calm name Will Larson - He had a chat on Lenny before.

This book elaborates 2 problems that I love and met before

  • How to scale a company effectively (1-10)

  • And how to solve productivity in team - teams (0-1 and 1-10)

Read 1/3 of the book so far and growing strong, but this gets tired too quickly, I have a habit of writing things down in simple terms so 50% of the reading time is for exacting the core ideas into another Notion page.

And I got bored again

So you know, once I get tired I switch to another book, more than I like to. Usually, a reading “phase” of mine will have 3 to 4 books at once.

The other one now is “The Right Stuff”, as a way to clear my mind from a technical book, I read a novel. This book was written by Tom Wolf, some kind of a legend when he coined the term “The me decade” - a term that became common as a descriptor for the 1970s (USA USA USA!).

In contrast to the 1960s USA, the decade is marked by individualism, something that more resonate to present-day America. Leave behind an era of post-war reconstruction, the average America now grow up in a safer, more prosperous, and all-powerful country - except for the fact that they sucked in Vietnam War, the Cold War, and the LSD (mixed feelings about this).

But now, a great time to live with the hippies and music, the generation of “now” is self-determinate, confident with a stronger tendency to pursue self-development and abandon old values - Hey, why is this sound like, hmmm, Gen Z?

  • Oh boy, history does repeat itself. YOLO!

Anw, this book is, well, crazy. With a dedication of roughly dozens of pages to demonstrate the death of the pilot, for real, it becomes a routine for our main character to join the funerals and on the other side, a wife that glooms with the thought of one day, her husband gonna be next.

But they do it, they do it anyway, with the great danger in the air, they still go out there and fly the shit out of their testing jet, thrusting into the horizon in search of “the right stuff”. Something that pushes them higher and faster, a calling that is true for every person on Earth to pursue.

It is just the right thing to do for them.

Still reading but love the idea, also there is a movie based on the book, gonna watch it later.

Some nice things, things are nice

What a life it was!

I found a book called “Đường Đời” (Life) that tells a story, an incredible one that is based on a real person's life. https://vi.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nguyễn_Hữu_Khai

Yeah, yeah, Vn-ese is my first language, okay so back to the man here, his life is one dark tunnel, so dark that even at the end, comes up another darker tunnel.

Let me recap

  • 15 yo, joined the army (Northside I assume) then got into one of the most brutal battles in the war - the first or the second Quang Tri battle.

  • Got injured then left school for good, and went to China to learn medicine (To cure his sister's blindness).

  • Catch up in another war in the book (1979).

  • Open a medicine company in HCMC.

  • Actually did it, became the chairman of the company, and then got arrested (he got arrested twice in a lifetime in the book).

    • His case was controversial also, look it up.

Someone to look up to, a good life. Highly recommend reading the Wiki page if you can read it, just want to keep the story alive, you are only dead when people stop remembering you.

Revisiting an old place

And then in the same coffee/book store, also lay The Universe in a Nutshell - Not sure how many times I read it, but I’m not sure if I ever finished the book, but I’m sure not remember it, my dumb mind started jumpy again.

Try to read it again. Or not. Or yes? Not sure but hey, elementary quantum physics for ya, read or not can be at the same time.

That’s it for now, what’s on your mind lately?
