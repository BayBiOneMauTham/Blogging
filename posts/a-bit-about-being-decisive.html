A bit about being decisive

Make fast decision doesn’t equal being right.

A bit about being decisive

Decisive is having or showing the ability to make decisions quickly and effectively, most of the time we skip the “effectively part”.

I’m always jokingly say, being decisive is different from being correct, you just have to decide. But how do you decide matter, outcome can be good, bad, ugly, but the way you approach the problem mean something. Especially when data is not available.

Last week

So as you might or might not know, I’m gradually taking the title of “senior” citizen

With that close to the name, I revisit the career path, now with a look up to director or head of product level, it just make sense to have a look to where you are going. Start thinking like one can help you know what your manager are expected in you also.

You can check out the video, yup, most of my learning start with Youtube then spread into further reading.

One thing in particular struck me, this slide

=> So you are telling me a higher level manager make decision from thin air but require lower level to be data-driven???

Do we go data-driven or do we want to make decision with less, this feel like a weird thing to even think about since in every interview you probably have as a Product Manager at any level, data analytics is required, not needed, but required.

But something you gotta learn to unlearn.

Last several years

This pull me back to several years, when I tried to find a MBA to pursuit. There was or still is a movement to anti the MBAs that only making decisions based on data, after all, that what they are trained for and we are in big-data age, nothing wrong with that.

  • But I do ask the questions: how decisions were made before the data-driven age? What are the rate of failure and did we built all this with lack of data decision?

I couldn’t find the answer, sadly, but I do remember that I took that argument into an interview (with a data platform company, lol) and got a silence reply.

For me the data-driven approach can have some problems

  • Human are, well, irrational rational creature, we are full of bias and have tendency to pick what fit to our idea.

  • Data size can be troublesome, if you can’t get most of the data, how can you say a data is true. For example, building a 1 million user app, how many user you understand to begin with? Probably 100-200 is a very very good number, and with that only 1/10.000 of the total user you serve.

  • Lack of understanding of data-processing, imagine having 10 million of data rows, how do you process them for insight? How did you collect and figure what to collect to begin with, are those approach right enough?

    • Most of the data in real-life I see are either (1) Collect everything and hope that maybe some make sense, or (2) Collect too little data and don’t have a way to retrieve historical data for analytics. Maybe the worst of all (3) have some data and tried to use slightly correlation to make sense of a decision.

Again, this is not me dismiss the decide-with-data approach, this is me heavy against decide-when-enough-data-only approach. Am still are, prove me wrong.

So on a higher level, you will need to make decision with little information and this raise a huge question - How do one become more decisive?

Yesterday

So I began to read - Thinking in bets by Annie Duke - with a very different idea in mind, I thought the book meant to show you how to make decision with data.

But yeah, you never know what you gonna get, I got around 100+ e-books without knowing what inside, sue me. I began to read like that ti find the unknown unknown, if you only read within your safe wall, you won’t know what outside.

Anyway, the book instead teaches you how to get comfortable with uncertainty and make better decisions as a result in “the ultimate guide to thinking about risk” (Bing said that not me). It put you in a different perspective and contradict to what you were taught.

Something from the book:

  • Chess isn’t a game theory example, it mostly based on skill without luck involve, basically a form of exact calculation.

  • Poker is luck-driven and closer example to real life situation, well, the unpredictability is what life give you, not lemon i guess.

To think about it for a second, every steps you take in life is no different from poker game, or a bet, as everything in life have some unpredictabilities.

  • If I want to go to work in LA, I will think about all the possibilities and outcomes with every piece of information that you have now

  • Placing a bet is no different, the only distinct is the amount of information, but again (how do you know what amount of information is more sufficient anw?), think in broader term, if uncertainty is still a factor, you have the same % of failure. 50/50.

I might over exaggerating it but take another example:

  • Coin flip, if you flip 9 tails in a round, would you rather bet that the 10th time be head or tail? The answer is still 50/50. Even if I tell you the odd is 99% head on 1% tail, in wider sense, 1% of 10 million flips is 100.000 tails.

This book also say that try to separate decision making process to the outcome, as outcome usually interfere with evaluate the decision. I’m not entirely sure on this part though, along with some of the methods raised but I guess it’s still a great thing to have a counter argument, especially in tech where most of the people I know obsess with data, me included sometime.

Being Head of Product or CPO are still out of my reach now but I think, people are people, wiser or smarter we are all human and so we prone toward error. Zoom out a little bit, it’s all 50/50 so why not try this way to compliment the others. The world need more data-informed not data-only leader.

And I’m might just be one.
