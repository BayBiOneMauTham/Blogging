Start with a “why?”

July 31, 2026 4 min read Your Name

Start with a “why?”

Many of the people I know are not doing this, they don’t reinvest and just keep grinding for personal gain. But the minority and most successful people have it differently.

I heard a story from a top Sales manager once. Whenever she travels abroad, she usually spends half the time there shopping, not for herself but for the customer she works with. One time it was buying the customer's baby shoes. Customers in plural.

I don’t know about you, but I have a hard time remembering shoe sizes in general, not mentioning their baby sizes. That what make the Sale manager one of the best in what she was doing.

This resonate also to my family, the best one usually spent a lot (Not just money, but time and effort) on their work, it not like they are doing that and expect something back, but rather these people do believe in nurturing and make others life better, being genuinely care also that propel them toward in their career.

They also put an extensive amount of effort into reading, learning, and exercising. Essentially building up personal capacity to do things and prepare for the future.

So re-investing is actually one of the trait I see from the top-notch people, if you wanna success it a yes, but is it apply to anyone?

Who should “bring the donut“?

When your job involves multiple parties to work with, this is usually the case.

Let’s start with a Youtube video as usual. I came across this couple of years ago, the woman in this was a “hostess”, essentially working to make others happy, a weird job but no judgment here, everyone has their way.

The point is she spent 40% of the income as giving back to her customers. Shocking as it might seem, I actually know others who practice this, and mostly the successful ones.

Working as a Product Manager, meaning you influence instead forcing people to do the thing you want (This should apply to everyone TBH). Quoting Ken Norton here

PMs are more humble servants than “CEOs of the product.” They put their teams first, they do what needs to be done, and they demonstrate that every day.

And that

If PMs don’t bring donuts for the team on launch day, who else will?

By the end of the day, the one who asked for the result should bear the responsibility to give back to the working team. Appreciate but also give something, people like the thank you but love the return values (And it not just the thing money can buy).

Being extra of everything help

Extra in time and attention (You can do this)

Take extra attention, extra time, be genuine, be there for others. On every troubled time I was there, staring at the spearhead.

While working for a start-up back in 2021, I used to sit with the development team from 7 AM to 10 PM in important weeks, not because I can improve the productivity (I barely know how to code), but because being myself there, just asking team-mates if I can help is good enough sometime.

Be genuine, I wasn’t there because I wanna push for my gain, it was morale boosting and always be the last man standing speak volume to your character.

Extra in your spending (Not for everyone)

On every release I invited the team for a mini celebration, on every sprint it was milk tea or coffee on the house, roughly 15% of my total income is spent on my working team/colleagues, a bit much for some but worth it for me. Others know I wasn’t the highest paid person in the room but usually be the most generous.

My other 5% is for meetups, usually coffee talk. I always take time to find the right place for the right people, and pay if you are the one who invites, it's a decent thing to do.

Another 15% of my income is on learning new things, being the smartest/calmest/most experienced in the room. Once you are there others shall know who to trust when problems occur, that projecting power in silence.

Everything in this also takes time, so again, do the extra.
